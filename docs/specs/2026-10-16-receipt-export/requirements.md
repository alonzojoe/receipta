# Receipt Export - Requirements

> **Amended after the layout redesign. Read "Amendment after the layout redesign" at the end
> before relying on the document sections below.** The author redesigned the receipt
> mid-build; the header, type scale, item table, amount, statement wording and payment row
> below describe the first design and are kept as written, not edited, so the change is
> visible. Criteria **1, 2, 4, 10 and 11 are superseded** (restated as 1', 2', 4', 10', 11'
> in the amendment); 6, 13, 19, 38 and 40 are amended in wording; the other criteria stand.
> Validation results are in `validation.md`.

## Goal

The receipt form collects everything and produces nothing. This spec is the other half of
Phase 1 and the whole of Phase 2: a designed receipt document, shown as a preview on the
Review step, that downloads as a **PDF** or an **image**. It is the reason the product
exists. A seller finishes the wizard and has something deliberate to send to a client over
Messenger, Viber or email, instead of a screenshot of a payment app.

The document is always black on white. It is a piece of paper, not a screen of the app, so
a receipt exported from dark mode is identical to one exported from light mode. It shows
who issued it, who it is for, what was sold (with the item photos), how it was paid, the
Receipta mark, and a plain statement that it is not a BIR Official Receipt.

Receipta is not a BIR Official Receipt. This spec adds the first artefact a user can hand
to a third party, so it is the first place that line can be crossed in the real world. The
document carries the statement on every export, with no switch to turn it off.

## The document

### Reading order and the reasoning

> **Superseded in part (amendment A1, A3).** The eye path is now title, receipt number and
> date, the two parties, then the amount as one sentence.

A receipt is read for **who it is from** and **how much**, in that order, then for what and
how. The layout is a single column so that order is the vertical order. There is no
two-column grid of metadata, because a reader should never have to decide where to look
next.

Eye path: issuer name (largest bold text on the page, top left) then the amount paid
(largest number, directly below) then the recipient, then detail.

### Blocks, top to bottom

> **Superseded (amendment A1 to A4, A7).** Blocks 1 and 2 (issuer-first header, summary row
> with a 36 px amount) and block 3 (name, `qty x price` subline) were redesigned. The table
> below is the first design.

| # | Block | Content | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Header** | Left: caption "Issued by" (13, muted, uppercase, tracked), then the **issuer name** (24, bold, wraps). Right-aligned: "RECEIPT" (13, accent, uppercase, tracked), then **Receipt No.** and **Date**, each as a small muted label over its value. | The issuer is the first thing and the biggest thing. Receipta's own mark is deliberately **not** here (see Branding below). |
| 2 | **Summary row** | Left: caption "Issued to", then the **recipient name** (18, semibold, wraps). Right-aligned: caption "Amount paid", then the **total** (36, bold, tabular figures). A hairline rule above and below. | The amount is the largest number on the page. The row wraps, amount below name, when a long name leaves no room, so neither is ever truncated or overlapped. |
| 3 | **Items** | A caption row "Item" and "Amount". Then one row per item: optional **thumbnail**, **name** (15, medium, wraps), beneath it a muted line `2 × ₱150.00`, and the **line amount** right-aligned (15, tabular). A hairline between rows. | Two columns plus a thumbnail, not a four-column table. A table with separate quantity and unit price columns is too tight at 600 px with a name that can be 100 characters, and does not survive being viewed at 0.65 scale. The `qty × price` subline keeps all three numbers a reader needs to check the line. |
| 4 | **Totals** | Right-aligned. With a discount: Subtotal, Discount (`-₱50.00`), then Total (20, bold) over a heavier rule. Without: Total only. | The Total here and the amount in block 2 are the same value from the same function. |
| 5 | **Payment** | Caption "Paid with". Institution **logo (28 px)** and name, or "Cash", or the Other text, alone. Below it, **Payment Reference No.** if present and the method is not Cash. | One place for payment. The date is already in the header. |
| 6 | **Notes** | Caption "Notes", the text with line breaks preserved (`pre-wrap`), 15, muted. **Omitted entirely when empty**: no caption, no gap. | Same rule as Review. |
| 7 | **Statement** | One line of body-size text, see "The not-a-BIR statement". | Repeated in the foot band of every earlier page of a multi-page PDF. |
| 8 | **Footer** | A rule, then "Generated by" followed by the Receipta mark and light wordmark. | See "Branding and the footer". Last page only. |

Text never truncates. Every name and note wraps (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`). A 100-character
name, a 5-line note and a 50-item receipt all lay out without clipping or overlap.

### Item images

**A thumbnail in the item's line, 56 x 56 px, cover-cropped, 6 px radius, 1 px rule border.
No gallery. No larger view.**

| Considered | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Thumbnail in the line | **Chosen.** The photo's job on a receipt is to say *which* item this line is. A small picture beside its name does that. It also stays attached to its price, so there is no hunting between a gallery and the list. |
| Gallery after the items | Rejected. It separates picture from line, forcing the reader to match them by order, and it adds up to 10 more image-heights to the document. |
| Large image per line | Rejected. 10 images at the stored 800 px are 10 pages of photo on a document whose point is the amount. They also multiply file size. |

- If **no item** has an image, the thumbnail column does not exist and the names sit flush
  left.
- If **some** items have images, every row reserves the 56 px column, so names align. A row
  without an image shows empty space, never a grey placeholder box: a placeholder reads as a
  broken image.
- The cap of 10 images means at most 10 rows carry the extra height. Image rows are 72 px
  tall including padding.
- Cover-crop discards the edges of a non-square photo. That is accepted for a thumbnail,
  and the original is not shown anywhere.
- `html2canvas-pro` support for `object-fit` is **to be verified**, not assumed. If it
  fails, the image is cropped to a square on a canvas before it is placed.

### Payment logo

**Shown, 28 px, beside the name.** The institution name is always present as text, so the
logo is not load-bearing, but it is what makes a GCash receipt look like a GCash receipt
and it is the reason the logos exist in the app at all. 28 px is large enough to recognise
at 0.65 scale (about 18 px) and small enough not to compete with the amount. The source
PNGs are 128 px, so 28 px at 3x (84 px) is downscaled, never enlarged. Cash and Other show
text only.

### The not-a-BIR statement

> **Superseded (amendment A6).** The sentence is now `This is a proof of payment.`, at the
> 13 px label size. The placement rule (every page) is unchanged; the reasoning below that
> depends on the words "not a BIR Official Receipt" no longer applies to the document.

**Wording, exact:**

> This is a proof of payment, not a BIR Official Receipt.

**Placement:** on a one-page receipt, and on the last page of a longer one, it is block 7,
immediately after the payment and notes and before the footer rule, in the document's
normal 15 px body size, muted ink. **On every earlier page of a multi-page PDF it is
repeated in that page's foot band** (see Pagination), at the same size.

**Every page, not only the last.** A page is what gets forwarded, screenshotted or printed
on its own. The claim that this is not an Official Receipt has to travel with whichever
page leaves the conversation, so it cannot live on one page of three. The Receipta footer
credit is different: it is branding, not a claim, so it appears once, on the last page.

Why here and not in the footer: footer fine print is where a disclaimer goes, and people
skip it. Placed straight after the money, at body size, it is read as part of the document,
which is the honest outcome. Why not boxed, uppercase, red or prefixed with "NOTICE" or
"DISCLAIMER": that makes the whole document read as a disclaimer, which is the other thing
the author does not want. It is one plain sentence, the same voice as the rest of the
product.

Why "proof of payment": that is the mission's own description of what Receipta produces,
and it tells the reader what the thing *is* as well as what it is not. A bare "not an
official receipt" tells them only what to refuse. The sentence is always present and has no
setting to remove it. Receipt No. is labelled "Receipt No.", never "OR No." or "Serial No.".

### Branding and the footer

> **Amended (amendment A5).** The footer is unchanged. The Receipta mark now also sits at the
> top right of the title row, which the paragraph "Why the logo is in the footer, not the
> header" below argued against.

**Footer text, exact:** `Generated by` followed by the Receipta logo (mark 20 px tall plus
`wordmark_light.webp` at 16 px tall, the same pairing as the navbar, `Logo` in
`src/shared/ui/logo.tsx`, light variant only). The wordmark is the word "Receipta", so
the sentence reads "Generated by Receipta".

- **No URL.** Receipta has no deployed address recorded anywhere in the repository; the
  only URL is the GitHub repository, which is a developer page, not a product page. A wrong
  or dead link on a document is worse than none. Add the address when one exists.
- **No timestamp.** "Generated 16 Oct 2026, 3:42 PM" would conflict with the receipt date,
  would age badly, and makes two exports of the same receipt differ, which would defeat the
  pixel comparison used to verify the white background.
- **No slogan, no "no account needed".** The footer is the author's footer, not an
  advertisement on the seller's document.

**Why the logo is in the footer, not the header.** The header belongs to the issuer. A
seller sends this to their own customer as their own document; leading it with someone
else's brand makes it look like Receipta's receipt. A "generated by" credit with the mark
is the normal place for a tool's brand. This is the decision the author is most likely to
want to flip; moving it is a one-block change.

**Branding constraint.** The document uses `LogoMark` and the light wordmark only. It must
not render `LogoWordmark`, because that component also renders the dark wordmark and relies
on a `dark:` class to hide one of them. In a dark-mode export that class would show the
near-white wordmark on white and hide the dark one, which is the exact leak the white
background criterion exists to catch. Use the two image sources directly, or a light-only
variant.

## Export

### PDF

**A4 portrait, paginated.**

| Considered | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| A4 portrait, paginated | **Chosen.** A4 is the Philippine standard for documents. It prints and attaches predictably. Pagination means the page count is the only thing that grows with the receipt, never the legibility. |
| A narrow receipt-width page, one long page | Rejected. It looks right on a phone, but a non-standard page size prints at an unpredictable scale, is handled inconsistently by chat previews and PDF viewers, and a 50-item receipt becomes a page several metres tall. |
| A5 | Rejected. At A5 the 600 px document would have to shrink to about 70%, putting the 13 px floor near 9 px, or be redesigned narrower, which breaks the single source of truth. |

**The honest cost.** A short receipt sits at the top of an A4 page with white below. That is
how an invoice from any office suite looks and is accepted. The other cost is real: opened
on a phone, an A4 PDF shows the 600 px document at roughly 0.65 scale, small. That is why
the **image is the phone path** and the PDF is the print and email path.

**Mapping.** 600 px of document = 210 mm of page width. The same scale applies vertically.
Each page is 849 px tall in document terms, with a **40 px top and bottom margin**, so
769 px is usable.

**The PDF paginates automatically.** The author's decision: a receipt with too many items
for one page becomes as many pages as it needs. The user chooses nothing and is not asked.
A 50-item receipt never drops items and never shrinks the type to fit one page.

**Row heights are not uniform, so pagination is computed from measurement.** A row with a
photo is at least 72 px; a row without one is about 48 px; a 100-character name that wraps
to three lines is about 96 px. A fixed rows-per-page number is the obvious wrong
implementation and fails as soon as one item has a photo and another does not, or one name
wraps. The implementation **measures each block and each item row in the rendered
capture node** (rounding each height **up** to a whole pixel, so a bottom border is never
clipped), and a **pure function** takes those heights and the page constraints and returns
the page breaks. It is a pure function precisely so the boundary cases below can be tested
without a canvas.

**Rules.**

- **A line is never split.** A row's name, `quantity x price` line, amount and thumbnail
  stay together on one page. Breaks happen only between rows or between blocks, never
  through a row, thumbnail or line of text. A row that does not fit in the space left moves
  to the next page whole. (A single row can never be taller than a page: the worst case, a
  100-character name plus a thumbnail, is about 100 px against 769. A test asserts this
  with maximum-length input, so a future change to the limits cannot silently break it.)
- **Greedy fill.** Rows fill a page until the next row would not fit, then a new page
  starts. A row that fits **exactly** stays on the page: the comparison is `<=`, not `<`.
- **The totals are never orphaned. Decision: the last item row travels with the totals,
  payment, notes, statement and footer as one unit.** The alternative, letting the totals
  start a page of their own under a continuation header, was rejected: a page with only
  totals has no line items to reconcile against, so a reader of that page alone cannot
  check the sum, and it is exactly the page most likely to be forwarded as proof of the
  amount. Moving one row costs a few pixels of white on the previous page. The unit is at
  most about 520 px (a long name on the last row, a 5-line note, a discount) and always
  fits on a page that carries a continuation header (76 px) and the page indicator.
- **Page 1** carries the full header and summary row (blocks 1 and 2), as on a one-page
  receipt.
- **Every later page carries a compact running header, rendered in the document's own
  font, not drawn by jsPDF:** the issuer name (15, semibold, wraps), `Receipt No.` and
  date, and `Page N of M`. It does **not** repeat the recipient, the amount, or the logo.
  Reasoning: a page separated from the rest must still say *whose receipt and which
  receipt*, and the issuer name, Receipt No. and date are enough to match it to its
  siblings. Repeating the full header and the amount on page 3 spends about 200 px of
  every page, and it makes three pages each look like a complete receipt, which is the
  wrong impression of a continuation. A bare page 3 is not identifiable. The compact
  header is the point between them. Because it is rendered by the same component, issuer
  names with any characters are drawn correctly; nothing depends on jsPDF's built-in font.
- **Every page of a multi-page PDF carries a page indicator, spelled out: `Page 1 of 3`,
  never a bare numeral**, so a recipient knows whether they hold the whole document. A
  one-page PDF has no indicator because there is nothing to be missing.
- **Every page except the last has a foot band** at the bottom of the usable area, with the
  statement (`This is a proof of payment, not a BIR Official Receipt.`) and
  `REF-20261016-154212 - Page 1 of 3`. On the last page the statement is in-flow (block 7)
  and the indicator sits beneath the footer. The Receipta credit (block 8) appears on the
  last page only.
- The foot band is about 64 px and the running header up to 76 px, so the space for rows is
  about 705 px on page 1 after the header blocks are accounted for by measurement, and about
  629 px on a continuation page. These are budgets for the implementer to check against the
  real measurement, not numbers to hard-code.
- Because the indicator needs the final page count, layout is **two-pass**: measure and
  paginate first, then render each page with its header, foot band and indicator.
- **Image rows and 50 items.** A receipt with 50 items and 10 photos is expected to take
  about 6 or 7 pages; 50 items without photos about 4. Both numbers are recorded in the
  criteria, not asserted by the spec.
- Per-page raster at **pixel ratio 3** (1800 px across the 600 px width, about 218 dpi on
  A4). Text is a raster, so it is not selectable or searchable. That tradeoff was accepted
  in `tech-stack.md`.
- PNG slices, not JPEG. JPEG leaves artefacts around small text on white, which is nearly
  the entire document.
- Metadata title `Receipt REF-...`. No author, no party names.

### Image

**PNG, 1200 px wide, 2x of the 600 px document, as one image of the full height.**

- **Why 1200 px.** It is about the physical pixel width of a mainstream phone screen, so a
  receipt opened full screen in a chat app is shown at roughly one image pixel per screen
  pixel, sharp. Wider adds file size without being visible on a phone. Chat apps also
  recompress and downscale large images; **I have not verified Messenger's and Viber's
  limits**, and the acceptance criteria record a real send, not an assumption.
- **Why 2x and not the 3x in `tech-stack.md`.** That mitigation was written for print, and
  it applies to the PDF here. The image is a chat asset. 3x gives 1800 px, a larger file,
  and visibly nothing more on a phone, and it moves a 50-item receipt toward the canvas size
  limits below.
- **Why PNG.** The mission says PNG for chat apps, and PNG keeps text crisp. The cost is
  size for receipts with photos; recorded in the criteria.
- **One image, full height. The image export does not paginate.** An image has no pages.
  A 50-item receipt is one tall image, about 5,400 px of document at 2x, roughly 10,800 px
  tall. It is not cropped, not split into several pictures, and not scaled down to fit a
  ratio: a multi-image export was not asked for, and the person on the other end would have
  to reassemble it.
- **The practical limit, stated.** A very tall image is scaled by chat apps to fit the
  screen width, which is fine, but many apps also cap the pixel height or preview only the
  top of it, and a long receipt is a long scroll after a tap. Whether that is a real
  problem for this audience is **unknown and unverified**; it is recorded in Deferred
  rather than answered with an invented feature. The PDF is the right output for a long
  receipt and the Review step does not hide it.
- **Canvas limits.** Mobile Safari caps a canvas at roughly 16.7 million pixels. At 2x, the
  tallest receipt is about 10,800 px high by 1200 wide, 13 million. That is under the limit
  with little margin, so the export computes the area first and **lowers the ratio until it
  fits** rather than failing, never below 1.5x. If it still does not fit, it fails loudly
  (see Failure).
- Full-bleed white: `backgroundColor: '#ffffff'` is passed explicitly, so the PNG has no
  transparent pixels regardless of the node.

### Filenames

`receipta-REF-20261016-154212.pdf` and `receipta-REF-20261016-154212.png`.

Brand first, so the file is findable in a phone's Downloads folder among `IMG_` and
`Screenshot_` files; the Receipt No. second, which carries the date and time and so is
unique and sorts chronologically. **No party names.** A name would put personal data into a
filename that sits in Downloads, shows up in share sheets and recent-file lists, and
needs sanitising for Unicode, length and illegal characters. The extension is the only
difference between the two, so a user who exports both sees the pair.

### Capturing the node

- The export captures a **separate, unscaled instance** of the document, mounted
  off-screen in a fixed container only while exporting, `aria-hidden` and inert. It is never
  the scaled preview, because html2canvas handles CSS `transform` badly and a scaled node
  would export at the wrong size.
- Before capture it waits for: fonts (`document.fonts.ready` plus loading the weights the
  document uses), and every `<img>` in the document to **decode**. A capture that begins
  before an image decodes is the usual cause of an export that is missing a photo.
- Request settings: `useCORS` off and `allowTaint` off. Every image is a data URL or a
  same-origin asset, and a tainted canvas cannot be exported anyway.
- No network request is made by export. All logos are same-origin; all item images are data
  URLs; the font is bundled.
- Download by object URL and a temporary anchor, revoked after use. The PDF uses jsPDF's save.

## Review step and the preview

The Review step changes from a plain summary into **the preview of the real document plus
the export actions**. This overrides the form spec's decision that Review is a plain themed
summary with no export control.

| Decision | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preview | **Yes. The actual document**, shown on the Review step, before any download. | The user is about to send this to a client. They should see it first. The form spec's Review step existed to catch a wrong amount or a misspelt name; the preview catches the same errors and also shows the layout, the photos and the statement. |
| The old summary | **Removed**, replaced by the preview. | Keeping both is two renderings of the same data that must be kept in sync and can disagree. The document shows every field the summary did, formatted as it will be sent. |
| Edit links | Kept, as a single row above the preview: "Edit: Parties, Items, Payment". | The summary's per-section Edit links are the only part of it worth keeping. The document itself has no links, since it is exported. |
| Straight download without preview | Rejected. | An export button on a bare summary invites sending a receipt whose layout the user never saw, with a photo possibly cropped in a way they did not expect. |
| Existing notice | "This is a receipt, not a BIR Official Receipt." is kept above the preview, unchanged. | The page, as well as the document, carries the line. |

**Preview scaling.** The document is 600 px wide. In the preview it is wrapped in a fluid
container and scaled down with a CSS transform to fit the width, never up. At 768 px and
above it shows at 100%. At 320 px the scale is about 0.48. That is too small to read body
text, which is a real weakness: the amount (36 px, about 17 px displayed) and the issuer
(24 px, about 11.5 px) stay readable, and the user can pinch-zoom, which the page must not
disable. A full-size viewer is deferred, not hidden: the weakness is stated.

**Actions.** Two buttons below the preview: **Download PDF** and **Download image**, side by
side from 640 px, full width and stacked below that, 44 px tall minimum. Back stays. Both
are disabled while either is running, because two simultaneous canvas renders is the
fastest way to run a phone out of memory. While running, the active button's label changes
to "Preparing PDF..." or "Preparing image...", it has `aria-busy`, and focus stays on it.

**Feedback on success.** A `react-hot-toast` with "PDF created" or "Image created", plus a
polite live region with the same text. The wording is "created", not "saved": a browser
decides where a download goes and on iOS it may open a viewer instead, so claiming "saved to
Downloads" would be a claim the app cannot know.

## Failure

The standard from the draft work applies: a silent failure is the worst outcome. A user who
taps Download, sees nothing, and sends the client nothing is the case to prevent.

- Every export is wrapped. **No code path ends with no file and no message.**
- A failure shows a **persistent inline message** beside the buttons (`role="alert"`), and
  a toast. The inline message stays until the next export attempt starts, so the user who
  looked away still sees it.
- The message states the cause where it is known and a next step, in plain language:

| Cause | Message |
| --- | --- |
| An item photo could not be drawn | "The photo for item 3 could not be read. Remove it and attach it again, then try again." |
| The canvas is too large or memory ran out (`toBlob` returns `null`, allocation throws) | "This receipt is too large to make as an image on this device. Try fewer photos, or download the PDF." |
| The export did not finish in 30 seconds | "Making the file took too long. Try again." |
| Anything else | "The file could not be created. Your receipt is safe. Try again." |

- The raw error goes to `console.error` for debugging. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Never hand over a wrong file.** Before download, the output is checked: non-zero size,
  and not blank (a fully white canvas is a failure, since a real receipt has text). A file
  that fails the check is not downloaded.
- **Degrading assets.** A failed **institution or Receipta logo** does not block export:
  the name or the words "Generated by Receipta" still carry the meaning. The export proceeds
  and the success message adds "The {name} logo could not be loaded." An **item photo**
  failure is not degraded, because dropping a photo silently changes the content.
- A **font** that fails to load falls back to a system sans-serif stack chosen to contain
  the peso sign. The export proceeds. The content is right and only the typeface differs.
- A failure never touches the form values, the draft, or the current step. The buttons are
  re-enabled and Retry is the same button.

## Money

- Every amount in the document comes from the **same pure functions** the form uses
  (`entities/receipt` money module): the line amount, subtotal, discount and total, and
  `format_centavos`. The document has no arithmetic of its own and no second formatter.
- Each line is rounded half-up once, lines sum to the subtotal, the discount subtracts,
  and nothing is rounded again. The printed line amounts always sum to the printed subtotal.
- A view-model function builds every displayed string from `ReceiptFormValues` once, so
  the document component only lays text out. That is what makes the next criteria
  testable without a canvas.
- Quantity prints with up to three decimals and no trailing zeros (`1.5`, `2`, `9,999.999`).
- The discount prints as `-₱50.00` with an ASCII hyphen-minus: the Unicode minus sign is
  not confirmed to exist in the bundled font subset, and a fallback glyph in the middle of
  an amount is exactly the visible flaw to avoid.
- The peso sign (U+20B1) must exist in the font as loaded, and is verified in a real export
  (see criteria). If the bundled Geist subset lacks it, the document uses the system
  stack for the whole sheet, never a single fallback glyph mixed into Geist digits.

## White-background integrity

A theme override that only *happens* to work in light mode is not white. Requirements:

- No file that renders the document uses a `dark:` variant, an app theme token class
  (`bg-background`, `text-foreground`, `border-border`, `bg-card`, `text-muted-foreground`,
  and any similar), or `currentColor` inherited from the page. Every colour is a
  `--receipt-*` property or a literal hex.
- The document root sets its own `background`, `color`, `color-scheme: light` and font, so
  nothing is inherited from `<html>` or `<body>`.
- Verification, in three independent layers:
  1. **A source test** that reads the document's files and fails on a `dark:` variant or a
     theme token class.
  2. **A browser computed-style walk**, with `.dark` set on `<html>`, over every element in
     the capture node: each `background-color` is transparent or `#FFFFFF`, and each
     `color` is in the document palette.
  3. **A pixel comparison**: export the same draft once in light mode and once in dark
     mode. The two PNGs decode to **identical pixels** (zero difference), and every pixel
     has alpha 255. The footer wordmark is the near-black one in both.
- The PDF is checked the same way: the first page rendered to an image from the dark-mode
  export has a pure white page background and the same content as the light-mode one.

### In scope

- The receipt document component and its view-model, with all blocks above
- Palette custom properties in `index.css`
- Installing `html2canvas-pro` and `jsPDF`, loaded dynamically

### Out of scope

- **Anything that makes the document more official**: TIN, address, registration,
  permit numbers, signature lines, stamps, "Paid" watermarks, QR codes, or serial numbers

  Receipt.
- VAT or any tax line. Multi-currency. Accounts, sync, upload, or any network request.
- Payment processing. Receipta documents a payment that already happened.
- Issuer logo upload, themes, colour choices, or multiple templates
- Print button or print stylesheet. The PDF is the print path.
- Editing any form value from the preview
- A multi-image export, or choosing a page size or page count
- Selectable-text PDF (see `tech-stack.md`)
- Re-issuing the Receipt No.

## Risks to watch

Recorded because the planner and the implementer should be told which parts are most
likely to go wrong, not discover it.

1. **Rasterising a 50-item, 10-photo document on a phone.** The biggest risk. Canvas size
   limits, memory, and `toBlob` returning `null` are all real on mobile Safari, and the
   worst case here is about 13 million pixels against a 16.7 million limit. This is the
   reason the ratio is adaptive and the failure path is specified, and the first thing to
   measure on a real phone.
2. **`html2canvas-pro` rendering fidelity.** `object-fit`, a variable font, the peso glyph
   and WebP all need to be shown to work in real output, not assumed. `tech-stack.md` names
   `modern-screenshot` as the fallback with a different failure profile.
3. **The preview at 320 px.** Scaled to about 0.48, body text is not legible until the user
   zooms.

## Deferred

Recorded so they are not mistaken for omissions.

- **Very tall images in chat threads.** A 50-item receipt is one image about 10,800 px
  tall. Chat apps may cap height or preview only the top. Unverified. If it proves a real
  problem, the answer is to be decided then (a hint toward the PDF, or an optional split),
  not invented now. Recorded, not solved.

- **Share to Messenger or Viber** via the Web Share API with the file. The best follow-up:
  it removes the step from Downloads to the chat. Not here, because support varies by
  browser and it is an addition to the output, not a requirement for it.
- **A full-size viewer for the preview** on a phone.
- **A repeated item-table header** on PDF continuation pages. The running header gives
  context and rows are self-explanatory.
- **Selectable-text PDF.** Accepted in `tech-stack.md`.
- **Same Receipt No. on two different documents.** Editing and re-exporting a draft gives
  one number to two documents. A "new receipt" prompt after export, or re-issuing on edit,
  would address it. Needs the author's decision because it changes the form's flow.
- **A product URL in the footer**, when one is deployed.
- **A timestamp in the footer.**
- **A smaller PNG** (palette quantisation) if photo-heavy receipts prove too large to send.
- **Per-user choices** of paper size, language or template.
- **Pixel-regression snapshots in CI.** The pixel comparison here is a manual, recorded
  check.
- **Copy review** of the statement and error messages by someone other than the implementer.

### The consequence of A6, stated plainly
